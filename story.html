The problem

Four syllabi describe four courses. None of them describes your semester.

Course A's midterm is fine. Course B's project is fine. All four landing inside the same seventy-two hours is not fine, and no document you own says so. You find out in week eleven, during week eleven.

So the product stopped being a parser and started answering one question:

The week of October 14 is your heaviest: a midterm, two assignments and three readings, across three courses.

What testing changed

Users refused the two features every product adds.

Kano classification put account creation and forced calendar sync in the same bucket: Reverse Needs. Not low priority. Actively unwanted.

Honouring that removed the login, then the server, then the backend entirely. Syllify now runs start to finish inside the browser tab. The constraint users imposed turned out to be the architecture.

The hard part

Four of eleven syllabi contain no dates at all.

Not badly formatted dates. None. They are numbered by session or teaching week, and the calendar lives in the professor's head. One of them is the syllabus for the course this project was built in.

A tool that assumes a date column fails on a third of real syllabi. A tool that guesses when it can't find one fails silently, which is worse.

Nearly half are only guesses, and the product says so instead of hiding it. Every row links to the exact line it came from. An AI pass runs on every upload, and anything it proposes is discarded unless it cites a line that actually supports it.

What I got wrong
  • We tested the mockup, not the mechanism.

    Two rounds of user testing on a Figma prototype came back 100% positive. The first time the real parser met real syllabi it found 39% of the deadlines. Nobody has to do anything to say yes to a picture.

  • A review found 30 defects in one pass.

    Six of them made the product state something untrue, which for a tool selling honesty is the only category that really matters. They're listed in the full report rather than quietly fixed.
